About the Game

Sea Power: Naval Combat in the Missile Age is a real-time naval combat game created by Triassic Games, a multinational development team that features air, sea, and land combat with in-depth modeling of ships, aircraft, and land units, realistic graphics, accurate sensor behavior, and full control of units. The game features single missions, short campaigns, and a mission editor, with the ability to upload or download missions to or from the Steam community workshop.

The game was released on November 12, 2024 on Steam and as of the time of writing (August 2025) currently enjoys an 89% positive user rating.

Features

  • Real-time combat with the ability to pause at any time
  • Vast array of detailed 3D modeled ships, submarines, aircraft, and land units and installations
  • Weapons and sensors that are (mostly) accurately modeled to real-life capabilities (although this is a video game, not a simulator)
  • Engage with units from the US, USSR, Japan, East and West Germany, Iran, and many other nations in a 1950's- 1980's setting
  • Play scenarios based on real-life events, alternate history, or make up your own!
  • Play different linear campaigns that allow a sense of progression, with a dynamic campaign in development
  • Use the Task Force Mode to build your own task force to fight during a campaign, allowing you to add, maintain, and swap units
  • No arbitrary unit limits in missions; have as many units as you want (or until your CPU melts!)
  • Extensive mod support for both scenarios and new units
  • Easy accessibility to modify game files to your heart's content

Q & A

Q: Do I have to know much about naval combat to be able to play the game?

A: No, but if you know very little, your learning curve will probably look something like this. Some information is more practical, and that should be the emphasis. You don't for example need to know the difference between X- and P-band radars, but knowing when you should and shouldn't use your radars is important.

Q: How long does a mission usually take?

A: It depends on the mission and how much time dilation you use. More complicated missions with many ships and different objectives will take longer than a skirmish between a few ships, or a submarine duel if you use heavy time dilation. But I would say most missions take 1-1.5 hours even with some time compression. Fortunately saving and loading were added to the game, which makes it possible to stop and come back later, which is a life saver, especially for longer missions.

Q: How well does the game run?

A: The developers have been doing a good job optimizing the game in order to increase performance. That being said, during development they suggested having a computer with a dedicated graphics card, which I feel is appropriate. The game also calculates lots of variables in a short time, so having a decent CPU is also helpful. With a Ryzen 5 7600 CPU and a 3060 ti graphics card I get about 70 FPS starting the mission, dropping down the longer the game runs. It usually gets down to 15-25 FPS after about an hour of playing the same mission, regardless of how many units are in the game. There is a memory leak that contributes to the decreasing performance that is known to the developers and should hopefully be fixed sometime soon.

Q: How much does the game cost? Where is the game sold?

A: The game is $50.00 on steam. It occasionally goes on sale, including during the steam seasonal sales. You can check the price history on steamdb here.As of now, The game is only sold on steam.

Q: Is there multiplayer in the game?

A: Currently there is no multiplayer. The developers have mentioned that they will consider adding it when the singleplayer elements are considered finished. There is little information as to whether multiplayer would be PvP or Co-op.

Q: What is the modding scene like? Are there any necessary mods?

A: The game is easily moddable and modders have been working since day 1 on different mods. Several of them are user-made scenarios, many of which are good and are definitely worth your time. Many mods are also kitbashes of in-game units and custom models of units that aren't in the game. Personally I prefer more realistic mods in the game's time frame so haven't really dug into most of those mods. There aren't any necessary mods in my opinion, but some that are popular are nuclearstonk's NTU and Pact Enhanced Lethality mods, some mods that change aircraft carrier cycle times, and some user-made campaigns.

Q: Are there any tutorials or walkthroughs that I can use to help me gain a better grasp of the game's concepts?

A: Yes! As of September 2025 the developers added a few tutorials in the game itself that goes over basic concets of how the game works and what you can do to make it easier to settle into the game, even if you don't know much about naval combat or are unfamiliar with naval strategy games.

Q: Are there any plans for DLC or expansions?

The developers mentioned in an AMA on the games first anniversary that they're considering a DLC for 1990's era weapons. They did not go into detail what exactly that would look like or what ships or aircraft would be a part of that. From their comments in the same AMA it seems that the Royal Navy will be a free addition to the game, and that other European navies, such as the French, West German, and Italian navies are also being considered for addition, again likely free updates to the game and not part of DLC. The British/Royal Navy has been confirmed to be coming, although other European navies are so far unconfirmed.

Q: What navies are in the game?

The main navies are the US and Soviet navies, with ships from the East and West German, Norweigan, Iranian, Libyan, Chinese, Japanese, and other navies. Many navies are also represented in name, operating ships built by another country, like Poland and Egypt operating Soviet ships, India operating old British ships, and so on. There are also many neutral and civilian ships and aircraft in the game from various countries, which can be used to make missions more interesting by forcing you to identify units before engaging them or risk suffering the consequences of attacking a neutral ship.

Q: What is the state of the game?

The game is still in Early Access after about a year after release. There have been a lot of additions to the game, both in new units and navies but also new quality of life and UI updates, as well as performance optimizations. The developers have stated that they are focused on improving the AI in the game and believe it will be a while before the dynamic campaign is added. As I mentioned in the performance question, there is still a lot of ground to cover in terms of optimization, but the game is very playable in its current state and a lot of fun. There has also been progress made in reducing micromanagement, although aircraft still require a lot of attention, and it gets to be a lot dealing with air assets along with maintaining situational awareness of the rest of your forces.

Q: What is the dynamic campaign supposed to be?

The dynamic campaign is still a WIP and so the final details are unknown but the general idea from what the developers have stated is that there will be a strategic map where you move forces around, and you or the enemy AI can initiate actions, bringing you into the mission mode where you fight with the relevant units in a certain area. A developer mentioned that you will be able to create your own formations and will be aiming to achieve certain objectives based on the side you play as. The campaign is currently planned to be based in the Atlantic Ocean between the US and USSR in the mid-1980s, with other campaigns in other areas and/or time periods possible but not planned. According to the game's roadmap and the developers, the campaign should be released by the end of the 2026.

Glossary

A glossary of some of the terms and acronyms used

  • AAA - Anti-Aircraft Artillery: Another way of saying anti-aircraft guns, artillery that is purpose-built to shoot down aircraft.
  • AEW - Airborne Early Warning: An aircraft-based radar system designed to detect enemy forces through radar and give friendly forces time to react to a threat. Synonymous with AWACS (Airborne Warning and Control System).
  • ASW - Anti-Submarine Warfare: The use of ships, aircraft, and submarines to hunt submarines, using torpedoes, depth charges, missiles, and sonobuoys.
  • CIWS - Close-In Weapons System: A weapons system that is designed to engage targets close to the ship it is mounted on. Considered the last line of defense against missiles, aircraft, and small craft. Early systems were 20 or 30 mm gatling guns, while modern systems include small missiles or lasers.
  • EW - Electronic Warfare: The use of electronic systems such as decoys or radar jamming to degrade an enemy's combat capability or to increase your own chances for success.
  • EMCON - Emissions Control: Aka "radio silence," preventing the use of radar or radio in order to prevent an enemy from discovering the existence of friendly forces through intercepting or detecting radio and radar use.
  • MAD - Magnetic Anomaly Detector: A device used to detect small variations in the Earth's magnetic field, in this case for the purpose of detecting submarines, whose steel hulls can create such a variation.
  • NATO - North Atlantic Treaty Organization: A military alliance between the USA, Canada, and several Western and Central European nations. Was aligned against the Warsaw Pact during the Cold War.
  • NBC - Nuclear-Biological-Chemical: A way of designing and protecting a ship and it's crew to make it more resistant to nuclear, biological, or chemical attack.
  • PLAN - People's Liberation Army Navy: The official name of the navy of communist China.
  • SAM - Surface-to-air Missile: A missile fired from a ship or a launcher on land that engages air targets, especially missiles and aircraft.
  • SEAD - Suppression of Enemy Air Defense: A tactic that focuses on suppressing or eliminating hostile radar sets and SAM sites. Best done with a combination of EW, anti-radiation missiles, and airstrikes.
  • VLS - Vertical Launch System: A method of launching a missile vertically instead of using a rail launcher. A VLS usually allows for more missiles to be carried, has a much faster reaction time than a rail launcher, and allows all the ship's missiles to be available immediately.
  • Warsaw Pact: A defensive alliance between the Soviet Union and several Eastern European communist countries. Analogous to NATO, which they were aligned against.
